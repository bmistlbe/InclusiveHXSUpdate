In this section, we present the ingredients entering the recommendation for
the inclusive Higgs-boson production cross section in gluon fusion (ggF).
The dominant QCD contribution is evaluated through N$^3$LO in the heavy-top
limit (HTL) and supplemented by the known finite-quark-mass and electroweak
corrections. Table~\ref{tab:ggf_cite} summarises the principal calculations,
PDF determinations and codes entering the recommendation or used in assessing
its theoretical uncertainties.

The partonic cross section is organised through N$^3$LO as~\cite{Dulat:2018rbf}
\begin{align}
\sigma_{ij} = &
\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO} C^2 \left(
\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},\mathrm{HTL}}
+ \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO},\mathrm{HTL}}
+ \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NNLO},\mathrm{HTL}}
+ \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^3\mathrm{LO},\mathrm{HTL}} \right) \nonumber \\
&+ \delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},(t,b,c)}
+ \delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO},(t,b,c)}
+ \delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t,b,c)}.
\label{eq:ggf_master}
\end{align}
Here, $\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$ accounts for the exact leading-order
top-quark-mass dependence, while the coefficient $C$ incorporates the QCD
matching coefficient together with the electroweak and mixed QCD--EW
corrections. The remaining terms restore the known finite top-, bottom- and
charm-quark mass dependence beyond the rescaled HTL approximation. These
ingredients are discussed in Sections~\ref{sec:ggf_htl}--\ref{sec:ggf_mq}.

For the central prediction we set $\mu_R=\mu_F=m_H/2$; the scale-variation
prescription is specified in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_htl}.
%In addition to the common inputs specified in Section~\ref{sec:setup}, we use
%a central renormalisation and factorisation scale
%\begin{equation}
%\mu_R=\mu_F=\frac{m_H}{2},
%\end{equation}
%and estimate the perturbative scale uncertainty by varying the common scale
%$\mu_R=\mu_F$ by factors of two around this central value.
The quark-mass
inputs are those specified in Eq.~\eqref{eq:qmasses}.

The uncertainties entering the recommendation are grouped into three
categories,
\begin{align}
\delta \sigma ={}&
\delta(\mathrm{theory})
+ \delta(\mathrm{PDF}+\alpha_s)
+ \delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH}),
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
\delta(\mathrm{theory}) &=
\delta(\mathrm{scale})
+ \delta(\mathrm{EWK})
+ \delta(t,b,c),
&
\delta(\mathrm{PDF}+\alpha_s) &=
\sqrt{\delta(\mathrm{PDF})^2+\delta(\alpha_s)^2}.
\end{align}
The theory uncertainty $\delta(\mathrm{theory})$ collects uncertainties
associated with missing or approximated fixed-order contributions. The
finite-quark-mass component $\delta(t,b,c)$ is defined and assessed in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_mq}. The PDF and $\alpha_s$ uncertainties are evaluated
using the prescription specified in Section~\ref{sec:pdf}. Finally,
$\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ accounts for the mismatch between the
N$^3$LO hard-scattering calculation and the NNLO PDFs used for the baseline
prediction. Its definition, together with the impact of using approximate
N$^3$LO PDFs, is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}. The impact of
QED evolution in the PDFs is treated separately in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_qed_pdf}.

Threshold-resummation effects beyond N$^3$LO are small
\cite{Anastasiou:2016cez,Bonvini:2014joa,Catani:2014uta} and are not included
in the central recommendation. Approximate N$^4$LO results in the HTL are
also available in the soft-virtual approximation~\cite{Das:2020adl}. These
do not enter the recommendation, but are used in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_n4lo_sv} as a check of the perturbative stability of the
N$^3$LO result.

\subsection{N$^3$LO Heavy-Top Limit Results}
\label{sec:ggf_htl}

The contributions
$\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},\mathrm{HTL}}$ with
$n=0,\,1,\,2,\,3$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_master} are computed in an effective
theory in which the top quark is integrated out
\cite{Kramer:1996iq,Chetyrkin:1997un,Schroder:2005hy,Chetyrkin:2005ia}.
The inclusive cross section in this theory is known at
NLO~\cite{Dawson:1990zj,Djouadi:1991tka},
NNLO~\cite{Anastasiou:2002yz,Harlander:2002wh,Ravindran:2003um}, and
N$^3$LO~\cite{Anastasiou:2015vya,Anastasiou:2016cez,Mistlberger:2018etf}.

The leading dependence on the finite top-quark mass is retained by rescaling
the HTL result with the exact top-only Born cross section. The corresponding
factor is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}
=
\frac{\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},(t)}}
     {\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},\mathrm{HTL}}},
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},(t)}$ denotes the full-theory result with a
top-quark loop~\cite{Georgi:1977gs,Wilczek:1977zn}. Finite-quark-mass
corrections beyond this Born rescaling are included separately as described
in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_mq}.

The HTL contributions used for the predictions in this report are evaluated
with \texttt{iHixs2}~\cite{Dulat:2018rbf}. Independent implementations are
available in \texttt{n3loxs}~\cite{Baglio:2022wzu} and
\texttt{ggHiggs}~\cite{Ball:2013bra,Bonvini:2014jma,Bonvini:2016frm,
Ahmed:2016otz,Bonvini:2018ixe,Bonvini:2018iwt}.

As specified above, we use the central scale
$\mu_R=\mu_F=m_H/2$ and estimate the uncertainty from missing higher-order
QCD corrections by a three-point variation of the common scale
$\mu=\mu_R=\mu_F$ by factors of $1/2$ and $2$. This differs slightly from
the default scale variation implemented in \texttt{iHixs2}, for which the
lower variation point is $\mu=0.4\,m_H$; that choice was introduced to obtain
a more conservative scale variation~\cite{Dulat:2018rbf}.

The top-quark mass scheme requires some care in applying the scale variation.
For the pure HTL contribution, the dependence on the top-quark mass enters
only through the Wilson coefficient $C$ and the resulting scale uncertainty
is rather insensitive to the mass scheme. The Born-rescaled result, however,
also depends on the top-quark mass through $\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$. In the
on-shell scheme the mass entering $\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$ remains fixed
under scale variation, whereas in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme the
running top-quark mass induces an additional scale dependence. This
dependence is anti-correlated with the scale variation of the HTL
contribution and consequently reduces the scale-variation band of the
Born-rescaled result. We therefore use the on-shell top-quark mass for the
central recommendation. The resulting scale uncertainty of the rescaled HTL
prediction is close to that of the pure HTL result. As an additional check
of the perturbative stability, the N$^3$LO prediction is compared with the
available approximate N$^4$LO HTL result in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_n4lo_sv}.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The Born-rescaled HTL contribution is included through N$^3$LO, with the
top-quark mass in $\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$ renormalised in the on-shell
scheme. The uncertainty $\delta(\mathrm{scale})$ associated with missing
higher-order QCD corrections is obtained from the three-point common-scale
variation described above. Finite-quark-mass corrections beyond the Born
rescaling, together with their associated uncertainties, are treated
separately in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_mq}.

\subsection{EW and Mixed QCD--EW Corrections}
\label{sec:ggf_ew}

The coefficient $C$ entering Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_master} combines the QCD
matching coefficient of the HTL effective theory with electroweak and mixed
QCD--EW corrections. It is written as
\begin{align}
C = C_\mathrm{QCD} + \lambda_\mathrm{EWK}
\left(1+\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}C_{1w}+\ldots\right).
\end{align}
Here, $C_\mathrm{QCD}$ is the QCD Wilson coefficient matching the HTL
effective theory to the Standard Model. It is known through
NNLO~\cite{Kramer:1996iq,Chetyrkin:1997un} and
N$^3$LO~\cite{Schroder:2005hy,Chetyrkin:2005ia}. The quantity
$\lambda_\mathrm{EWK}$ incorporates the leading electroweak correction
\cite{Aglietti:2004nj,Actis:2008ug}, while $C_{1w}$ parametrises the QCD
correction to the light-quark-mediated electroweak contribution.

The dominant electroweak contribution of this type arises from light-quark
loops coupling the initial-state gluons to electroweak gauge bosons, which in
turn couple to the Higgs boson. The corresponding mixed QCD--EW correction
was first obtained in the limit of large electroweak-boson masses
\cite{Anastasiou:2008tj}, yielding $C_{1w}=7/6$. Subsequent calculations
considered the opposite small-mass limit~\cite{Anastasiou:2018adr} and the
virtual corrections with exact $W$- and $Z$-boson mass dependence
\cite{Bonetti:2016brm,Bonetti:2017ovy,Bonetti:2018ukf,Bonetti:2020hqh,
Bonetti:2022lrk}. The complete NLO-QCD correction to the light-quark-mediated
contribution, including the real-radiation and quark-initiated channels with
the full electroweak-boson mass dependence, was subsequently calculated in
Ref.~\cite{Becchetti:2020wof}.

For our central scale choice, the treatment retaining the finite
electroweak-boson masses corresponds effectively to $C_{1w}\simeq-1.7$,
compared with $C_{1w}=7/6$ in the large-mass approximation. Using the
finite-mass result changes the total Higgs-production cross section by
approximately $-0.53\%$ for $m_H=125.09\GeV$ over the range
$\sqrt{s}=7$--$14$~TeV. The exact light-quark calculation therefore supports
the earlier estimate of the mixed QCD--EW correction and lies within the
$\delta(\mathrm{EWK})=1\%$ uncertainty assigned in
YR4~\cite{LHCHiggsCrossSectionWorkingGroup:2016ypw}.

Recent progress has provided the complete set of two-loop master integrals
required for the top-quark-mediated mixed QCD--EW contribution, retaining the
full dependence on $m_t$, $m_H$, $M_W$ and $M_Z$~\cite{Marzucca:2025eak}.
This constitutes an important ingredient towards a complete calculation, but
does not yet provide a corresponding inclusive cross-section prediction.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The central prediction includes the electroweak corrections of
Refs.~\cite{Aglietti:2004nj,Actis:2008ug}. For consistency with the
established implementation used for the predictions in this report, the
mixed QCD--EW correction entering the central value is evaluated using the
large-electroweak-boson-mass approximation of
Ref.~\cite{Anastasiou:2008tj}. The calculation with exact electroweak-boson
mass dependence~\cite{Becchetti:2020wof} shows that the resulting shift in
the total cross section is contained within the assigned uncertainty.
We therefore retain
\begin{equation}
\delta(\mathrm{EWK})=\pm1\%
\end{equation}
as the uncertainty associated with missing and approximated mixed
QCD--EW corrections.

\subsection{Finite Quark Mass Corrections}
\label{sec:ggf_mq}

The Born-rescaled HTL contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_master} captures the
exact top-quark-mass dependence at LO but not the full finite-mass dependence
of the higher-order QCD corrections. We therefore supplement it with the
known corrections involving finite top-, bottom- and charm-quark masses.
Using the notation $\left[\ldots\right]_{\alpha_s^i}$ to retain only terms
proportional to $\alpha_s^i$, these contributions are defined by
\begin{align}
\delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},(t,b,c)}
={}&
\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{LO},(t,b,c)}
-\left[
C_\mathrm{QCD}^2 R_\mathrm{LO}\sigma_{ij}^\mathrm{HTL}
\right]_{\alpha_s^2},
\\
\delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO},(t,b,c)}
={}&
\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NLO},(t,b,c)}
-\left[
C_\mathrm{QCD}^2 R_\mathrm{LO}\sigma_{ij}^\mathrm{HTL}
\right]_{\alpha_s^3},
\\
\delta \sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t,b,c)}
={}&
\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t,b,c)}
-\left[
C_\mathrm{QCD}^2 R_\mathrm{LO}\sigma_{ij}^\mathrm{HTL}
\right]_{\alpha_s^4}.
\label{eq:ggf_delta_tbc}
\end{align}
The exact QCD corrections with full dependence on the quark masses are known
at LO~\cite{Wilczek:1977zn} and NLO
\cite{Graudenz:1992pv,Spira:1995rr}. At NNLO, exact results are available for
the top-only contribution
\cite{Czakon:2020vql,Czakon:2021yub,Czakon:2024ywb}
and for the top--bottom interference
\cite{Czakon:2023kqm,Czakon:2024ywb}, while the bottom-only, charm-only and
remaining charm-interference contributions are known through NLO. At
N$^3$LO, finite-top-mass information is presently restricted to virtual
corrections obtained as an expansion in $1/m_t^2$~\cite{Davies:2019wmk}.

It is useful to decompose the finite-mass correction according to the quark
coupling to the Higgs boson,
\begin{align}
\delta\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(t,b,c)}
={}&
\delta\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(t)}
+\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(b)}
+\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(c)}
+\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(t\times b)}
\nonumber\\
&+\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(t\times c)}
+\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(b\times c)},
\end{align}
where $\delta\sigma_{ij}^{\mathrm{N}^n\mathrm{LO},(t)}$ denotes the
difference between the top-only full-theory contribution and the
Born-rescaled HTL result, defined analogously to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_delta_tbc}.

The individual uncertainties associated with the finite-mass corrections are
assessed in the following subsections. The Higgs-mass extrapolation and
quark-mass and flavour-scheme dependences are considered explicitly, together
with uncertainties from missing higher-order corrections. The former are
found to be negligible at the precision of the present recommendation. The
finite-quark-mass uncertainty retained in the final prediction is therefore
\begin{align}
\delta(t,b,c) ={}&
\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t)
+\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t\times b).
\label{eq:ggf_mass_uncertainty}
\end{align}
The two contributions are combined linearly. This provides a conservative
estimate, since the scale variations of the top-only and top--bottom
contributions are found to be anti-correlated at NLO.

Table~\ref{tab:ggf_mtcorr} shows the finite-top-quark-mass corrections at
NLO and NNLO. The available NNLO results were obtained for the reference
Higgs-boson mass $m_H^\mathrm{ref}=125.09$\,GeV. To obtain predictions over
the full Higgs-mass grid of this report, we extrapolate the NNLO correction
using the Higgs-mass dependence of the corresponding Born-rescaled HTL
contribution. Defining
\begin{align}
R^H_n(m_H)
={}&
\frac{
\left[
C_\mathrm{QCD}^2 \mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}
\sigma_{ij}^\mathrm{HTL}
\right]_{\alpha_s^n}(m_H)
}{
\left[
C_\mathrm{QCD}^2 \mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}
\sigma_{ij}^\mathrm{HTL}
\right]_{\alpha_s^n}(m_H^\mathrm{ref})
},
\end{align}
we use
\begin{align}
\delta \sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t),\mathrm{ext}}(m_H)
={}&
R^H_4(m_H)\,
\delta \sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t)}(m_H^\mathrm{ref}).
\end{align}
A conservative estimate of the uncertainty associated with this procedure is
obtained by assigning a $100\%$ uncertainty to the extrapolation-induced
shift,
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{ext}(t)
={}&
\delta \sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t),\mathrm{ext}}(m_H)
-\delta \sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t)}(m_H^\mathrm{ref}).
\end{align}
At NLO, where the exact Higgs-mass dependence is known, the extrapolated and
exact results differ by at most $0.02\%$ of the total cross section, while
the assigned uncertainty is typically at least a factor of two larger. At
NNLO, comparison with the known $1/m_t^2$ expansion gives differences of at
most $0.07\%$. Across the Higgs masses and collider energies considered in
this report, the resulting extrapolation uncertainty never exceeds $0.03\%$
of the total cross section and is therefore neglected.

The dependence on the top-quark mass-renormalisation scheme is also small at
NNLO. Ref.~\cite{Czakon:2024ywb} compares the top-only contribution in the
on-shell and $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ schemes at $\sqrt{s}=13$\,TeV and
finds a difference of $-0.01$\,pb. Relative to the total cross section, we
take this to correspond to
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{scheme}(t)=\pm0.01\%,
\end{align}
which is neglected in the final uncertainty.

The dominant residual uncertainty associated with the finite-top correction
arises from missing perturbative orders. A seven-point scale variation of
the NNLO finite-top correction in Ref.~\cite{Czakon:2023kqm} gives
$^{+0.13}_{-0.03}$\,pb at $\sqrt{s}=13$\,TeV. Symmetrising this variation
and expressing it relative to the total cross section gives
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t)=\pm0.17\%.
\label{eq:ggf_t_mhou}
\end{align}
Over the collider-energy range $\sqrt{s}=7$--$14$\,TeV, the corresponding
uncertainty varies between approximately $\pm0.14\%$ and
$\pm0.18\%$~\cite{Czakon:2023kqm}. We therefore use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_t_mhou} as a common relative uncertainty for all collider
energies and Higgs-boson masses considered here.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The finite-top-quark-mass correction is included through NNLO in the central
prediction. The uncertainties associated with the Higgs-mass extrapolation
and the top-quark mass-renormalisation scheme are negligible at the precision
of the present recommendation and are not retained. We assign
$\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t)=\pm0.17\%$ for missing higher-order finite-top-mass
corrections and include this contribution in the total finite-quark-mass
uncertainty $\delta(t,b,c)$.

\delta \sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t\times b)}(m_H^\mathrm{ref}).
\end{align}
We estimate the associated uncertainty from the extrapolation-induced shift,
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{ext}(t\times b)
={}&
\sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t\times b),\mathrm{ext}}(m_H)
-\sigma^{\mathrm{NNLO},(t\times b)}(m_H^\mathrm{ref}).
\end{align}
At NLO, comparison with the exact Higgs-mass dependence shows that the
extrapolated result differs from the exact result by at most $0.06\%$ of the
total cross section, while the assigned uncertainty is typically larger by a
factor of about three. Across the collider energies and Higgs-boson masses
considered here, the NNLO extrapolation uncertainty never exceeds $0.01\%$
of the total cross section and is neglected.

The choice of flavour and bottom-mass renormalisation schemes has been studied
in detail in Ref.~\cite{Czakon:2024ywb}. We adopt the 5FS with the bottom-quark
mass renormalised in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme. In this setup the
perturbative series exhibits good convergence and the NNLO correction is
small. At $\sqrt{s}=13$\,TeV, the difference between the 5FS and 4FS results,
using an $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ bottom mass in both cases, is
$-0.09$\,pb. Symmetrising this difference and expressing it relative to the
total cross section gives
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{scheme}(t\times b)=\pm0.09\%.
\end{align}
The agreement between the 4FS and 5FS results supports the stability of the
chosen flavour-scheme prescription, and this uncertainty is neglected in the
final recommendation.

For comparison, within the 5FS the difference between using an
$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ and an on-shell bottom-quark mass is substantially
larger, amounting to $-0.25$\,pb in the comparison of
Ref.~\cite{Czakon:2024ywb}. More importantly, the on-shell scheme exhibits
poorer perturbative convergence and a larger scale dependence. We therefore
do not use the difference between the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ and on-shell
mass schemes as an additional uncertainty; rather, the
$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme is adopted as the perturbatively better
behaved prescription.

The residual uncertainty from missing higher-order corrections to the
top--bottom interference is estimated by scale variation. At
$\sqrt{s}=13$\,TeV, the seven-point scale variation of the NNLO correction
reported in Ref.~\cite{Czakon:2024ywb} is
$^{+0.13}_{-0.03}$\,pb. Symmetrising this variation and expressing it
relative to the total cross section gives
\begin{align}
\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t\times b)=\pm0.17\%,
\end{align}
which we use as a common relative uncertainty for all collider energies and
Higgs-boson masses considered here.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The top--bottom interference is included through NNLO in the central
prediction, using the 5FS with an $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ bottom-quark mass
and an on-shell top-quark mass. The uncertainty associated with the
Higgs-mass extrapolation and the residual 4FS--5FS difference are negligible
at the precision of the present recommendation and are not retained. We
assign
$\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(t\times b)=\pm0.17\%$ for missing higher-order
corrections and include this contribution in the total finite-quark-mass
uncertainty $\delta(t,b,c)$.

\subsubsection{Bottom \& Charm Contributions
($\sigma^{(b)},\sigma^{(c)},\sigma^{(t\times c)},\sigma^{(b\times c)}$)}
\label{sec:ggf_mbmc}

The bottom-only, charm-only and charm-interference contributions are known
through NLO~\cite{Graudenz:1992pv,Spira:1995rr} and are included using
$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ masses for the bottom and charm quarks. Their
numerical sizes at $\sqrt{s}=13.6$~TeV are shown in
Table~\ref{tab:ggf_tbc}. At NLO, the bottom-only, charm-only and
bottom--charm interference contributions account for approximately
$+0.21\%$, $0.002\%$ and $+0.04\%$ of the total cross section,
respectively. The top--charm interference is somewhat larger and gives a
correction of approximately $-0.45\%$.

The uncertainty from missing higher-order corrections is estimated by scale
variation. For the bottom-only contribution at $\sqrt{s}=13$~TeV and the
reference Higgs-boson mass, the three-point scale variation gives
\begin{equation}
\sigma^{(b)}=0.10^{+0.03}_{-0.02}\,\mathrm{pb},
\end{equation}
corresponding to
\begin{equation}
\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(b)=\pm0.06\%
\end{equation}
when expressed relative to the total gluon-fusion cross section. Combining
the charm-only and charm-interference contributions gives
\begin{equation}
\sigma^{(c)}+\sigma^{(t\times c)}+\sigma^{(b\times c)}
=-0.19^{+0.03}_{-0.02}\,\mathrm{pb},
\end{equation}
corresponding likewise to
\begin{equation}
\delta^\mathrm{MHOU}(c,t\times c,b\times c)=\pm0.06\%.
\end{equation}
These uncertainties remain at the per-mille level over the collider energies
and Higgs-boson masses considered in this report and are negligible compared
with the other uncertainties entering the recommendation.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The bottom-only, charm-only, top--charm and bottom--charm contributions are
included through NLO in the central prediction, with the bottom- and
charm-quark masses renormalised in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme.
Their missing-higher-order uncertainties are estimated by scale variation
and found to be negligible at the precision of the present recommendation;
they are therefore not included in the total finite-quark-mass uncertainty.

\subsection{PDF-Order Uncertainty and Impact of aN$^3$LO PDFs}
\label{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:an3lo-pdfs}, the baseline PDF4LHC21 set is
available at NNLO, while the hard-scattering contribution to gluon-fusion
Higgs production is known through N$^3$LO in the HTL. The use of NNLO PDFs
with an N$^3$LO matrix element therefore introduces a mismatch in
perturbative accuracy. In the baseline recommendation, we account for this
through an additional PDF-order uncertainty,
\begin{align}
\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})
=
\pm\left|
\sigma^{(2),\mathrm{HTL},\mathrm{NNLO}}
-
\sigma^{(2),\mathrm{HTL},\mathrm{NLO}}
\right|,
\label{eq:ggf_pdf_th}
\end{align}
where the superscript $(2)$ denotes the NNLO rescaled-HTL matrix element and
the final label denotes the perturbative order of the PDFs. The evaluation
uses the PDF4LHC15 combination~\cite{Butterworth:2015oua}, for which
consistent NLO and NNLO sets are available.

Equation~\eqref{eq:ggf_pdf_th} corresponds to twice the PDF-order uncertainty
assigned in YR4. The more conservative prescription is motivated by the
observed impact of replacing NNLO PDFs by the available approximate N$^3$LO
PDFs in the N$^3$LO Higgs cross section. As reviewed in
Section~\ref{sec:an3lo-pdfs}, independent aN$^3$LO determinations have been
produced by MSHT~\cite{McGowan:2022nag} and
NNPDF~\cite{NNPDF:2024nan}, and subsequently combined into the
MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO set~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}.

not be confused with the PDF-order effect isolated in the upper panel.

For completeness, the tables in Appendix~\ref{app:tables} also allow the
baseline PDF4LHC21 prediction to be converted to a prediction using the
MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO combination. The conversion is defined by
\begin{align}
\Delta^\text{aN3LO}
={}&
\sigma(\text{MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO\_qed})
-\left[
\sigma(\text{PDF4LHC21})
+\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}
\right],
\label{eq:ggf_pdf_conversion}
\end{align}
where the QED-evolution correction
$\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}$ is defined in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_qed_pdf}. It is important to note that, when using the
MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO prediction, the PDF+$\alpha_s$ uncertainty should be
taken from the corresponding aN$^3$LO tables and
$\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ should not be included, since there is no
longer a mismatch between the perturbative orders of the hard-scattering
calculation and the PDFs.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
The baseline recommendation uses the N$^3$LO HTL matrix element with the
PDF4LHC21 NNLO set, following the common setup of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}. The PDF+$\alpha_s$ uncertainty is evaluated using
the standard Hessian prescription through
LHAPDF~\cite{Buckley:2014ana}. We additionally assign the PDF-order
uncertainty $\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_pdf_th}. As an alternative, we provide predictions using
the MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO combination~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}; for these
predictions the PDF uncertainty is taken directly from the aN$^3$LO set and
no separate $\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ uncertainty is assigned.

\subsection{Impact of QED Evolution in the PDFs}
\label{sec:ggf_qed_pdf}

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:pdf}, QED corrections to DGLAP evolution
modify the quark and gluon distributions in addition to generating a photon
PDF. For gluon-fusion Higgs production, the relevant effect is primarily the
resulting modification of the gluon luminosity. Since this effect is at the
sub-percent level for the baseline PDF4LHC21 prescription, it is included in
the central prediction. Photon-initiated Higgs production is not included;
a fully consistent treatment of QED effects would require both contributions.

QED-extended NNLO PDF sets have been produced by the CT, MSHT and NNPDF
collaborations, using the LUXqed formalism
\cite{Manohar:2016nzj,Manohar:2017eqh} to constrain the photon distribution
\cite{Harland-Lang:2019pla,Xie:2021equ,Cridge:2021pxm,Cridge:2023ryv,
Bertone:2017bme,Barontini:2024dyb,NNPDF:2024djq}. QED-extended aN$^3$LO
sets are also available from MSHT and NNPDF
\cite{Cridge:2023ryv,Barontini:2024dyb}.

Since PDF4LHC21 itself does not provide a QED-evolved set, we follow
Ref.~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2025sqw} and estimate the corresponding correction
from the QED shifts of the three global PDF determinations entering the
PDF4LHC21 combination:
\begin{align}
\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}
={}&
\frac{1}{3}\bigl(
\langle\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{CT18})\rangle
+\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{MSHT20})
+\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{NNPDF3.1})
\bigr),
\label{eq:ggf_qed}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
\langle\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{CT18})\rangle
={}&
\frac{1}{3}\bigl[
\sigma(\text{CT18lux})
+\sigma(\text{CT18qed\_proton})
+\sigma(\text{CT18qed})
\bigr]
-\sigma(\text{CT18NNLO}),
\nonumber\\
\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{MSHT20})
={}&
\sigma(\text{MSHT20qed\_nnlo})
-\sigma(\text{MSHT20nnlo\_as118}),
\nonumber\\
\Delta^\text{NNLO}_\text{QED}(\text{NNPDF3.1})
={}&
\sigma(\text{NNPDF31\_nnlo\_as\_0118\_luxqed})
-\sigma(\text{NNPDF31\_nnlo\_as\_0118}).
\end{align}
The shift is evaluated using the Born-rescaled N$^3$LO HTL contribution and
expressed as a relative correction, which is then applied to the full
gluon-fusion prediction.

The individual PDF determinations show a non-negligible spread in the impact
of QED evolution. At $\sqrt{s}=13.6$~TeV, the NNLO shifts range from
$-0.05\%$ for CT18 to $-1.23\%$ for NNPDF3.1, while the PDF4LHC21
prescription gives $-0.63\%$. This spread reflects differences in how the
inclusion of QED evolution modifies the quark and gluon distributions in the
individual global fits, despite their common use of LUX-based information to
constrain the photon PDF.

For the aN$^3$LO alternative discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}, QED evolution is included directly through
the MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO\_qed set. We define
\begin{align}
\Delta^\text{aN3LO}_\text{QED}
={}&
\sigma(\text{MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO\_qed})
-\sigma(\text{MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO}).
\end{align}
At $\sqrt{s}=13.6$~TeV, this gives a $-1.45\%$ shift for the combined set,
compared with $-1.00\%$ for MSHT20 and $-1.65\%$ for NNPDF4.0. The QED
effect is therefore larger for the available aN$^3$LO sets than for the
PDF4LHC21-based NNLO prescription.

The impact of QED evolution on the PDF uncertainty of the PDF4LHC21
combination has not been evaluated separately. We therefore retain the
PDF uncertainty obtained from the QCD-only PDF4LHC21 set. The QED-induced
shift is included as a correction to the central cross section and no
additional QED-PDF uncertainty is assigned.

\paragraph{Recommendation}
For the baseline PDF4LHC21 prediction, the QED-evolution correction is
included according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_qed}, following
Ref.~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2025sqw}. For the alternative aN$^3$LO prediction,
QED evolution is included through the
MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO\_qed set. Photon-initiated Higgs production is not
included in either case.

The treatment of the perturbative scale uncertainty has changed relative to
YR4. In procedure F, YR4 obtained
$\delta(\mathrm{scale})={}^{+0.2\%}_{-2.4\%}$ from a common-scale scan over
the range
\begin{equation}
    m_H/4 \leq \mu_R=\mu_F \leq m_H,
\end{equation}
while procedure G used a symmetrised seven-point scale variation and obtained
an uncertainty of approximately $\pm3.0\%$. In the present recommendation,
the scale uncertainty is instead obtained from the three-point common-scale
variation described in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_htl}. In addition, YR4 used a
running $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ top-quark mass in the Born-rescaling factor
$\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$. As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_htl}, the
scale dependence of this running mass is anti-correlated with that of the HTL
contribution and reduces the resulting scale-variation band. The present
recommendation instead uses a fixed on-shell top-quark mass in
$\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{LO}$. The somewhat larger scale uncertainty in the
current recommendation should therefore not be interpreted as a deterioration
in the perturbative accuracy of the calculation.

The treatment of the electroweak and mixed QCD--EW corrections in the central
prediction is unchanged relative to YR4. We retain
$\delta(\mathrm{EWK})=\pm1\%$, corresponding to the F uncertainty assigned
in YR4. The subsequent calculation with exact electroweak-boson mass
dependence provides an additional validation that the approximation used in
the recommendation is covered by this uncertainty
\cite{Becchetti:2020wof}.

The treatment of finite-quark-mass effects has changed more substantially.
Exact NNLO results for the finite-top contribution and the top--bottom
interference are now included in the central prediction. Consequently, the
separate $\delta(1/m_t)$ uncertainty used in YR4 is no longer required, and
the residual finite-quark-mass uncertainty is reduced from $\pm0.83\%$ to
$\pm0.34\%$. As described in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_mq}, the latter consists
of the missing-higher-order uncertainties associated with the NNLO top-only
and top--bottom contributions. These are estimated by scale variation and
combined linearly. The remaining finite-mass extrapolation and scheme
uncertainties are found to be negligible at the precision of the present
recommendation.

The truncation uncertainty introduced in YR4 to account for the then
incomplete N$^3$LO HTL calculation is likewise no longer required following
completion of the N$^3$LO result~\cite{Mistlberger:2018etf}. Taken together,
the improved knowledge of the finite-mass and N$^3$LO corrections reduces
the corresponding components of the theory uncertainty, despite the more
conservative scale-variation prescription adopted here.

The PDF+$\alpha_s$ uncertainty decreases with the replacement of PDF4LHC15
by PDF4LHC21. Conversely, the PDF-order uncertainty
$\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ is increased from $\pm1.16\%$ in YR4 to
$\pm2.31\%$ in the present recommendation. As discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}, this more conservative prescription is
motivated by the sizeable shift observed when the N$^3$LO hard-scattering
calculation is convoluted with the available aN$^3$LO PDFs. Predictions
using the combined MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO set~\cite{Cridge:2024icl} are
therefore also provided as an alternative. For these predictions, no
separate $\delta(\mathrm{PDF\text{--}TH})$ uncertainty is assigned.

Overall, the advances since YR4 have removed several uncertainty components
associated with previously unknown perturbative corrections and reduced the
finite-quark-mass and PDF+$\alpha_s$ uncertainties. At the same time, the
present recommendation adopts more conservative prescriptions for the
residual scale dependence and for the mismatch between the perturbative
orders of the hard-scattering calculation and the PDFs.
