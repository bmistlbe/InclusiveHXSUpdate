Here we specify the common input parameters and baseline conventions used for
the predictions presented in this report. Unless stated otherwise in the
individual production-mode sections, the numerical inputs given below are used
throughout. Where applicable, these inputs were chosen following the Review of
Particle Physics (PDG)~\cite{ParticleDataGroup:2022pth}.\footnote{At the time
when this work was started, the 2022 edition of the PDG was the most recent.}
Any departures from this common setup are stated explicitly in the
corresponding sections.

\subsection{Fermion masses}
We use the following quark-mass inputs:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:qmasses}
&M_t^\mathrm{OS}=172.5\,\mathrm{GeV},&
&M_b^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(M_b^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}})=4.18\,\mathrm{GeV},&
&M_c^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(3.0\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.986\,\mathrm{GeV}.&
\end{align}
Quark widths are neglected. The mass-renormalisation schemes employed in the
individual calculations are specified in the corresponding production-mode
sections. When an $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ top-quark mass is required, we use
\begin{align}
M_t^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(M_t^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}})
=162.5\,\mathrm{GeV}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Gauge boson masses}

We use the following on-shell values for the $W$- and $Z$-boson masses and
widths:
\begin{align}
M^\OS_W &= 80.379\GeV\,, \qquad &\Gamma^\OS_W &= 2.085\GeV\,, \notag\\
M^\OS_Z &= 91.1876\GeV\,, \qquad &\Gamma^\OS_Z &= 2.4952\GeV\,.
\end{align}
When required for electroweak calculations, these on-shell parameters are
converted to the corresponding pole masses and widths~\cite{BARDIN1988539}
according to
\begin{align}
M_V &=
\frac{M^\OS_V}{\sqrt{1+(\Gamma^\OS_V/M^\OS_V)^2}}\,,
\qquad
\Gamma_V =
\frac{\Gamma^\OS_V}{\sqrt{1+(\Gamma^\OS_V/M^\OS_V)^2}}\,.
\end{align}

The electromagnetic coupling is defined in the $G_\mu$ scheme
\cite{Denner:2000bj}. In terms of the Fermi constant and the on-shell
gauge-boson masses, we use
\begin{align}
\alpha_{G_\mu}
&= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}G_F(M^\OS_W)^2\sin^2\theta_W\,,
&
\sin^2\theta_W
&=1-\frac{(M^\OS_W)^2}{(M^\OS_Z)^2}\,,
&
G_F&=1.16638\cdot10^{-5}\GeV^{-2}\,.
\end{align}
With the inputs above, this gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{G_\mu}=0.007565210\,.
\end{equation}

\end{align}     


\subsection{PDF and $\alphas$}
\label{sec:pdf}
Following the PDF4LHC
recommendation~\cite{PDF4LHCWorkingGroup:2022cjn} we use PDF4LHC21\_40
PDF set for all predictions. The value of the strong coupling,
$\alphas$, is given at the $Z$ boson mass
\begin{equation}
        \alphas(M_Z) = 0.1180\pm0.001\,.
\end{equation}  
We estimate the $\alphas$ and PDF uncertainties following the same
recommendation. The $4$-flavour version of the PDF is used whenever a
calculations is performed in the $4$-flavour scheme. The above
combined PDF sets do not contain any photon content, and at the time
when the setup was converged on, there was no recommendation for how
to handle the missing photon. Only very recently have the main PDF
collaborations published a procedure for how to include the photon PDF
is Higgs production~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2025sqw}. As discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ggf_qed_pdf} we have included these recommendations for
the gluon-fusion production mode. For the other production modes, When
computing photon initiated processes, we instead use the previous
PDF4LHC PDF set,
LUXqed17\_plus\_PDF4LHC15\_nnlo\_100~\cite{Manohar:2017eqh}, but
crucially for the photon content \emph{only}. This is not fully consistent
with using PDF4LHC21\_40 for the quarks and gluons, as the
presence of the photon in the PDF inevitably impacts the distributions
of all the other partons. However, since the photon-initiated
component is at the order of a few percent at most, this inconsistency
is expected to be fully contained within other theoretical
uncertainties even when assuming a $100\%$ uncertainty.


\subsection{Parton distributions beyond NNLO}
\label{sec:an3lo-pdfs}

The increasing perturbative accuracy of LHC cross-section calculations has
motivated a corresponding effort to extend global determinations of parton
distribution functions (PDFs) beyond NNLO. This is particularly relevant for
Higgs-boson phenomenology, since inclusive predictions for the dominant
gluon-fusion production mode, as well as for vector-boson fusion and the
Drell--Yan-like contribution to associated $VH$ production, are available at
N$^3$LO in QCD~\cite{Mistlberger:2018etf,Dreyer:2016oyx,Baglio:2022wzu}.
A formally consistent prediction at this order requires PDFs whose evolution
and determination are performed at the corresponding perturbative accuracy.
Until recently, however, several ingredients required for a complete N$^3$LO
global PDF analysis were only partially known. As a consequence, N$^3$LO
partonic cross sections have conventionally been convoluted with NNLO PDFs,
introducing a mismatch in perturbative accuracy.

A central ingredient in extending PDF determinations to N$^3$LO is the
four-loop splitting functions governing DGLAP evolution. Their calculation has
progressed substantially over the last decade. Early results determined
specific colour structures, large-$n_f$ contributions and the non-singlet
splitting functions in the planar limit
\cite{Davies:2016jie,Moch:2017uml,Moch:2018wjh}. Subsequently, low Mellin
moments of the four-loop splitting functions were calculated
\cite{Moch:2021qrk}, providing important constraints on the singlet evolution
matrix. This information was extended considerably in a series of calculations
of additional Mellin moments and exact contributions to the four-loop kernels
\cite{Falcioni:2023luc,Falcioni:2023tzp,Moch:2023tdj,
Gehrmann:2023cqm,Gehrmann:2023iah}. In particular, exact all-$N$ results have
been obtained for several fermionic contributions, while the increasing number
of known moments, together with constraints on the small- and large-$x$ limits,
has allowed increasingly precise approximations to the full $x$-dependence of
the splitting functions.

More recently, the available information on the phenomenologically important
singlet evolution has improved significantly. Higher Mellin moments of the
quark-to-gluon and gluon-gluon splitting functions have been calculated in
Refs.~\cite{Falcioni:2024xyt,Falcioni:2024qpd}. Combined with the known
endpoint behaviour, these results permit precise $x$-space approximations to
the corresponding four-loop kernels. The residual uncertainty associated with
these approximations is small over the range in $x$ relevant for most LHC
applications, and the resulting N$^3$LO contribution to the evolution of the
gluon distribution is at the percent level or below over a broad
phenomenologically relevant range. The status of the non-singlet sector has
advanced further still: following a number of exact partial results, the
complete four-loop non-singlet splitting functions have recently been
calculated analytically~\cite{Gehrmann:2026qbl}. Thus, while the full singlet
evolution kernels are not yet known analytically as functions of $x$, the
information presently available permits N$^3$LO evolution at a precision
suitable for current collider phenomenology. Comparisons between independent
implementations of approximate N$^3$LO evolution have also been performed,
providing an important validation of the approximations and their associated
uncertainties~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2024crx}.

A second essential ingredient is the treatment of heavy flavours. Heavy-quark
contributions exhibit scaling violations that differ from those in the
massless case~\cite{Blumlein:2016xcy}, and must be accounted for through the
corresponding heavy-flavour Wilson coefficients in deep-inelastic scattering
and other hard-scattering processes. Heavy-flavour corrections also determine
the matching of the parton densities across flavour thresholds in
variable-flavour-number schemes (VFNS), for both single-heavy-quark
\cite{Buza:1996wv} and two-heavy-quark cases~\cite{Ablinger:2017err}, through
the corresponding massive operator matrix elements (OMEs). Until recently,
these matching corrections were available only through two loops
\cite{Laenen:1992zk,Laenen:1992xs,Buza:1995ie,Buza:1996wv,
Bierenbaum:2007qe,Bierenbaum:2008yu,Bierenbaum:2009zt,Bierenbaum:2022biv}.
For the structure function $F_2(x,Q^2)$, the asymptotic description reaches
the $O(1\%)$ level for $Q^2/m_Q^2 \gtrsim 10$, cf.~\cite{Buza:1995ie}, a
region relevant to the cuts commonly employed in PDF fits to suppress
higher-twist contributions.

Progress towards the three-loop description began with the calculation of a
series of three-loop Mellin moments in Ref.~\cite{Bierenbaum:2009mv}. Over the
period 2009--2025, the three-loop contributions to the massive OMEs in the
unpolarized and polarized single- and two-mass cases were calculated, including
the corresponding asymptotic heavy-flavour Wilson coefficients for
deep-inelastic scattering. The different contributions were obtained in
Refs.~\cite{Ablinger:2010ty,
Ablinger:2014vwa,Ablinger:2014nga,Ablinger:2014lka,Behring:2014eya,
Ablinger:2017err,Ablinger:2018brx,Ablinger:2017xml,
Ablinger:2022wbb,Ablinger:2023ahe,Ablinger:2024xtt,Ablinger:2025joi,
Ablinger:2025nnq}; a survey of the mathematical methods developed for these
calculations can be found in Ref.~\cite{Blumlein:2023aso}. In the singlet
sector, the heavy-quark corrections to the structure functions are sizeable
and exhibit scaling violations that differ significantly from the massless
case. These results make it possible to perform consistent NNLO QCD
non-singlet analyses~\cite{Blumlein:2021lmf}, as well as singlet analyses with
a correlated treatment of $\alpha_s$, $m_c$, $m_b$ and the parton densities.
They also lift the VFNS matching from two-loop~\cite{Ablinger:2017err}
to three-loop order~\cite{Ablinger:2025joi}, providing the heavy-flavour
threshold matching required for N$^3$LO PDF evolution.
Fast numerical implementations of the three-loop single-mass OMEs and
the corresponding VFNS matching relations are publicly available through
the \texttt{libome} library~\cite{Ablinger:2025joi}. The numerical importance
of these corrections has recently been illustrated in the HOPPET
implementation of N$^3$LO evolution, which interfaces to \texttt{libome} for
the three-loop flavour-threshold matching~\cite{Karlberg:2025hxk}.
While N$^3$LO effects on light-flavour PDFs are generally at or below the
percent level over much of the LHC-relevant $x$ range, substantially larger
effects can occur for the heavy-quark distributions, driven predominantly by
the three-loop flavour-threshold matching corrections.

These advances in massless evolution and heavy-flavour matching form part of
the theoretical basis for global PDF determinations beyond NNLO. The first
global PDF determination incorporating approximate N$^3$LO information was
performed by the MSHT collaboration~\cite{McGowan:2022nag}. The resulting
MSHT20aN$^3$LO PDFs included the then-available four-loop splitting-function
information, N$^3$LO DIS coefficient functions and heavy-flavour
contributions, together with approximations for ingredients that were not
known exactly at the time of the fit. Unknown higher-order contributions are
parameterised and their associated uncertainties propagated into the
resulting PDFs.

An independent approximate-N$^3$LO determination was subsequently
presented by the NNPDF collaboration~\cite{NNPDF:2024nan}. In this
approach, missing higher-order uncertainties are incorporated through
a theory covariance matrix, while the available N$^3$LO information is
included in the PDF evolution and in the theoretical predictions
entering the fit. In particular, the treatment of heavy-quark mass
effects in deep-inelastic scattering is extended to this order within
the FONLL general-mass scheme~\cite{Forte:2010ta}. The two
determinations therefore provide complementary implementations of
global PDF fits beyond NNLO and different treatments of the
theoretical uncertainties associated with the remaining approximate
ingredients.

Since these first fits were performed, the theoretical information
entering N$^3$LO PDF determinations has continued to improve
substantially. In particular, additional information on the four-loop
splitting functions and newly determined three-loop transition matrix
elements have become available, reducing the reliance on
approximations in both DGLAP evolution and heavy-flavour matching. The
impact of these developments has been studied in an update of the MSHT
aN$^3$LO analysis
\cite{Cridge:2025oel}. The resulting changes in the PDFs are found to be at
most comparable to, and typically smaller than, the uncertainties assigned in
the original MSHT20aN$^3$LO fit, with still smaller effects on the corresponding
parton luminosities. This provides an important validation of the uncertainty
treatment adopted in the original approximate N$^3$LO determination. The
continued calculation of exact N$^3$LO ingredients is nevertheless important
both for further reducing these uncertainties and for ultimately replacing
the present approximate fits by fully consistent N$^3$LO PDF determinations.

The availability of independent MSHT and NNPDF aN$^3$LO sets has also made it
possible to construct a common combination. Following a detailed comparison
and benchmarking of the two determinations, the
MSHT20xNNPDF40\_aN3LO set was produced in
Ref.~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}. This combination provides a useful representation
of the present knowledge of PDFs at approximate N$^3$LO and allows the impact
of increasing the perturbative order of the PDFs to be studied independently
of the choice of a single fitting methodology.

These developments have direct implications for precision Higgs-boson
phenomenology. The transition from NNLO to aN$^3$LO PDFs changes both the
parton luminosities and the corresponding inclusive Higgs-boson cross
sections. This is particularly important for gluon fusion, for which the
N$^3$LO partonic calculation probes directly the gluon luminosity and where
the use of NNLO PDFs leaves a mismatch between the perturbative accuracy of
the hard scattering and the PDF evolution. Studies with the individual MSHT
and NNPDF aN$^3$LO sets, and subsequently with their combination, have shown
that the corresponding shift in the gluon-fusion cross section can reach
several percent at LHC energies
\cite{McGowan:2022nag,NNPDF:2024nan,Cridge:2024icl}. The size of this effect
is comparable to, and can exceed, the nominal PDF uncertainty, making the
perturbative accuracy of the PDFs an important component of the uncertainty
budget for precision Higgs predictions. Smaller but phenomenologically
relevant effects are also found for other Higgs-boson production modes
available at N$^3$LO~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}.

For the common setup adopted in this report, PDF4LHC21 at NNLO remains the
baseline PDF set, as described above. The availability of aN$^3$LO PDFs
nevertheless provides important information with which to assess the
uncertainty associated with combining N$^3$LO matrix elements with NNLO PDFs.
This issue is numerically most important for gluon-fusion Higgs production and
is therefore discussed in detail in Sec.~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}. There we
compare predictions obtained using the PDF4LHC21 NNLO set with those obtained
using the individual and combined aN$^3$LO PDFs, and discuss the implications
for both the central prediction and the treatment of the PDF-related
theoretical uncertainty.
