Here we specify the common input parameters and baseline conventions used for
the predictions presented in this report. Unless stated otherwise in the
individual production-mode sections, the numerical inputs given below are used
throughout. Where applicable, these inputs follow the Review of Particle
Physics (PDG)~\cite{ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz}.\footnote{The 2020 edition of the
PDG was the most recent one when this work was started.} Departures from this
common setup are stated explicitly in the corresponding sections, and the most
significant of them are collected in Section~\ref{sec:pdf}.

\subsection{Fermion masses}
We use the following quark-mass inputs:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:qmasses}
&M_t^\mathrm{OS}=172.5\,\mathrm{GeV},&
&M_b^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(M_b^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}})=4.18\,\mathrm{GeV},&
&M_c^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(3.0\,\mathrm{GeV})=0.986\,\mathrm{GeV}.&
\end{align}
Quark widths are neglected. The mass-renormalisation scheme used in each
calculation is specified in the corresponding production-mode section. Where an
$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ top-quark mass is required, we use
\begin{align}
M_t^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(M_t^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}})
=162.5\,\mathrm{GeV}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Gauge boson masses}

The $W$- and $Z$-boson masses and widths are taken on-shell,
\begin{align}
M^\OS_W &= 80.379\GeV\,, \qquad &\Gamma^\OS_W &= 2.085\GeV\,, \notag\\
M^\OS_Z &= 91.1876\GeV\,, \qquad &\Gamma^\OS_Z &= 2.4952\GeV\,,
\end{align}
and are converted, where an electroweak calculation requires it, to the
corresponding pole masses and widths~\cite{BARDIN1988539} according to
\begin{align}
M_V &=
\frac{M^\OS_V}{\sqrt{1+(\Gamma^\OS_V/M^\OS_V)^2}}\,,
\qquad
\Gamma_V =
\frac{\Gamma^\OS_V}{\sqrt{1+(\Gamma^\OS_V/M^\OS_V)^2}}\,.
\end{align}

The electromagnetic coupling is defined in the $G_\mu$
scheme~\cite{Denner:2000bj}. In terms of the Fermi constant and the on-shell
gauge-boson masses,
\begin{align}
\alpha_{G_\mu}
&= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}G_F(M^\OS_W)^2\sin^2\theta_W\,,
&
\sin^2\theta_W
&=1-\frac{(M^\OS_W)^2}{(M^\OS_Z)^2}\,,
&
G_F&=1.16638\cdot10^{-5}\GeV^{-2}\,,
\end{align}
which gives
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{G_\mu}=0.007565210\,.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Higgs masses and collider energies}
Predictions are provided on a grid of Higgs-boson masses in the range
$120\leq m_H\leq130\GeV$. The grid is chosen to resolve the region around the
measured value, and includes $m_H=125.09\GeV$, the ATLAS+CMS Run-1
combination~\cite{ATLAS:2015yey}, and $m_H=125.38\GeV$, as measured by
CMS~\cite{CMS:2020xrn}:
\begin{align}
m_H = \{&120, 122, 124, \notag\\
        &124.6, 124.8, 125, 125.09, 125.2, 125.3, 125.38, 125.6, \notag\\
        &126, 128, 130\}\,\mathrm{GeV}.
\end{align}

The centre-of-mass energies cover the legacy LHC analyses, the current
programme, and the HL-LHC:
\begin{align}
\sqrt{s}=\{7,8,13,13.6,14\}\,\mathrm{TeV}.
\end{align}
The only exception is $t\bar tH$ and $tH$ production, for which predictions are
given at $13$, $13.6$ and $14$~TeV; for the two lower energies we continue to
recommend the numbers of Ref.~\cite{Karlberg:2024zxx}.


\subsection{Parton distributions and \texorpdfstring{$\alphas$}{alpha\_s}}
\label{sec:pdf}

The baseline set for this report is PDF4LHC21\_40 at NNLO, and the strong
coupling is
\begin{equation}
    \alphas(M_Z)=0.1180\pm0.001\,,
\end{equation}
with PDF and $\alphas$ uncertainties evaluated following the PDF4LHC
recommendation~\cite{PDF4LHCWorkingGroup:2022cjn}. The PDF4LHC21 set is a Monte Carlo
combination of the CT18, MSHT20 and NNPDF3.1
sets~\cite{Hou:2019efy,Bailey:2020ooq,NNPDF:2017mvq}, reduced here to a
$40$-member Hessian representation. The working group takes the PDF4LHC
recommendation as its reference prescription, and PDF4LHC21 is the most recent
such recommendation; this, rather than a process-by-process optimisation, is
what fixes the baseline. It also explains why the use of parton distributions
beyond NNLO is a more delicate matter, since no PDF4LHC recommendation exists
at that accuracy; we return to this point in Section~\ref{sec:an3lo-pdfs}.
Whenever a calculation is performed in the four-flavour scheme, the
corresponding four-flavour set is used.

Two departures from this baseline should be stated plainly.

The first concerns $b\bar bH$ production, which does not use PDF4LHC21. A
consistent matching of the four- and five-flavour schemes requires the bottom
distribution to be evolved across the flavour threshold in a uniform way and
for every value of $m_b$ and of the matching scale $\mu_b$ entering the
matched prediction. The sets entering the PDF4LHC21 combination adopt
different values of $m_b$, so the combination itself cannot serve this purpose.
Dedicated sets are used instead, obtained by re-evolving the
\texttt{PDF4LHC15\_nnlo\_mc} input from an initial scale of $2\GeV$, as
described in Ref.~\cite{Bonvini:2016fgf} and in Section~\ref{sec:bbH}.

The second concerns the photon. PDF4LHC21\_40 carries no photon distribution,
so wherever photon-initiated channels are included in the electroweak
corrections we take the photon from
LUXqed17\_plus\_PDF4LHC15\_nnlo\_100~\cite{Manohar:2017eqh} while retaining
PDF4LHC21\_40 for the quarks and gluons. This hybrid is not internally
consistent: a photon distribution is inseparable from the QED corrections to
the DGLAP evolution, which also modify the quark and gluon distributions. It is
useful to keep the two effects apart. Photon-initiated partonic channels
contribute explicitly to the hard-scattering cross section, whereas QED
evolution changes the parton luminosities entering all remaining channels. The
prescription above accounts for the first and neglects the second.

It should be said that this prescription was adopted at an early stage of the
present work, before dedicated studies of QED effects in Higgs production were
available, and that it has been retained for the production modes whose
predictions were completed under it. The explicit photon-initiated contribution
is process dependent but numerically stable: for VBF at $\sqrt{s}=14$~TeV and
$m_H=125.09\GeV$ it amounts to $40.6\,\mathrm{fb}$, or $0.94\%$ of the total
cross section, and to $0.92\%$ and $0.93\%$ at $13$ and $13.6$~TeV
respectively. The neglected QED-evolution effect has since been quantified in
Ref.~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2025sqw}, which constructs a PDF4LHC21 QED correction
from the corresponding CT18, MSHT20 and NNPDF3.1 QED-enhanced sets and finds
shifts at $14$~TeV of $-0.7\%$ for VBF, $-0.6\%$ for $ZH$ and $W^+H$ and
$-0.55\%$ for $W^-H$. For VBF we have evaluated the same correction directly in
the setup of this report, obtaining $-0.63\%$ at $13.6$~TeV and $-0.63\%$ at
$14$~TeV, slightly smaller in magnitude than the value quoted above but of the
same size and sign. These are to be compared with a PDF$+\alphas$
uncertainty of $2.2\%$ and a total uncertainty of $2.4\%$ for VBF, and with
uncertainties of around $2\%$ for $W^\pm H$. The omitted effect is therefore
smaller than the uncertainties already assigned, and we do not introduce a
further uncertainty to cover it. This is not to say that the effect is
negligible, only that accounting for it would not change the central
predictions or their uncertainty budgets appreciably. We stress that the
photon-initiated contribution and the QED-evolution shift are distinct and
should not be treated as correlated; their partial cancellation in VBF is
incidental.

Gluon fusion is treated differently, and the report therefore does not apply a
single QED prescription throughout. There the cross section is known at
N$^3$LO, sub-percent changes in the gluon luminosity are not negligible against
the other uncertainties, and the QED-evolution shift is instead included
explicitly following Ref.~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2025sqw}; photon-initiated Higgs
production is not included in that case. The implementation and its numerical
impact are described in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_qed_pdf}. A uniform treatment of
photon-initiated contributions and QED evolution across all production modes
would be desirable in a future update.


\subsection{Parton distributions beyond NNLO}
\label{sec:an3lo-pdfs}

Inclusive predictions for gluon fusion, for vector-boson fusion and for the
Drell--Yan-like part of $VH$ production are available at N$^3$LO in
QCD~\cite{Mistlberger:2018etf,Dreyer:2016oyx,Baglio:2022wzu}, while the
baseline parton distributions defined above are determined and evolved at NNLO.
Of these, the gluon-fusion and vector-boson-fusion recommendations of this
report are built on the N$^3$LO results; the $VH$ recommendation is based on
the NNLO calculation, as described in Section~\ref{sec:VH}, and is therefore
not affected.
A formally consistent prediction would require both at the same accuracy. No
PDF4LHC recommendation exists beyond NNLO, so this mismatch cannot be resolved
within the prescription the working group follows, and we retain NNLO parton
distributions as the baseline. The mismatch is instead carried as an
uncertainty, which is numerically most important for gluon fusion and is
discussed, together with the alternative of using approximate N$^3$LO sets, in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}.

The ingredients needed to go beyond NNLO have nevertheless advanced
considerably in recent years, in the massless evolution kernels and in the
treatment of heavy quarks alike. For the four-loop splitting functions, even-$N$
moments of the quark-to-gluon and gluon-gluon kernels have been computed and,
combined with the known endpoint behaviour, turned into controlled $x$-space
approximations~\cite{Falcioni:2024xyt,Falcioni:2024qpd}; the resulting N$^3$LO
contribution to the scale derivative of the gluon distribution amounts to $1\%$
or less for $x\gtrsim10^{-4}$. The non-singlet sector is now known exactly: all
four-loop non-singlet splitting functions have been obtained in fully analytic
form~\cite{Gehrmann:2026qbl}. Independent implementations of approximate
N$^3$LO evolution in \texttt{APFEL++}, \texttt{EKO}, \texttt{HOPPET} and
\texttt{Candia}~\cite{Bertone:2017gds,Candido:2022tld,Karlberg:2025hxk,Hampson:2025pvi}
allow these ingredients to be cross-checked, and dedicated benchmarking of the
MSHT and NNPDF evolution codes against the FHMRUVV approximation finds
excellent agreement once common ingredients are
used~\cite{Cooper-Sarkar:2024crx,Bertone:2024dpm}.

Heavy quarks require separate treatment. Their contributions to the structure
functions exhibit scaling violations that differ from the massless
case~\cite{Blumlein:2016xcy}, and must be accounted for through the
heavy-flavour Wilson coefficients in deep-inelastic scattering and in other
hard-scattering processes. The same corrections fix the parton densities in
variable-flavour-number schemes (VFNS), in the single-mass~\cite{Buza:1996wv}
and two-mass~\cite{Ablinger:2017err} cases, through the associated massive
operator matrix elements (OMEs). For $F_2(x,Q^2)$ the asymptotic description is
accurate at the $O(1\%)$ level for $Q^2/m_Q^2\gtrsim10$~\cite{Buza:1995ie}, a
region matching the cuts usually imposed in PDF fits to suppress higher-twist
contributions. Until recently these corrections were known only to two
loops~\cite{Laenen:1992zk,Laenen:1992xs,Buza:1995ie,Buza:1996wv,
Bierenbaum:2007qe,Bierenbaum:2008yu,Bierenbaum:2009zt,Bierenbaum:2022biv}.
Starting from the three-loop Mellin moments of
Ref.~\cite{Bierenbaum:2009mv}, the three-loop contributions to the massive OMEs
were obtained over the period 2009--2025 in the unpolarised and polarised
single- and two-mass cases, together with the corresponding asymptotic
heavy-flavour Wilson coefficients~\cite{Ablinger:2010ty,Ablinger:2014vwa,
Ablinger:2014nga,Ablinger:2014lka,Behring:2014eya,Ablinger:2017err,
Ablinger:2018brx,Ablinger:2017xml,Ablinger:2022wbb,Ablinger:2023ahe,
Ablinger:2024xtt}; the two-mass contributions to
$\widetilde{A}_{Qg}^{(3)}$ of Ref.~\cite{Ablinger:2025nnq} complete the
calculation of all unpolarised and polarised massive three-loop OMEs. A survey
of the mathematical methods developed along the way is given in
Ref.~\cite{Blumlein:2023aso}. In the singlet sector the heavy-quark corrections
to the structure functions are large and their scaling violations differ
markedly from the massless case. These results make consistent NNLO non-singlet
analyses~\cite{Blumlein:2021lmf} and singlet analyses with a correlated
treatment of $\alphas$, $m_c$, $m_b$ and the parton densities possible for the
first time. They also lift the VFNS matching relations from two-loop to
three-loop order in the single-mass case~\cite{Ablinger:2025joi}, giving a
refined description of a massive quark becoming light at large virtualities,
and allowing heavy-quark parton distributions to be defined in that framework.

Two approximate N$^3$LO (aN$^3$LO) global determinations exist, from
MSHT~\cite{McGowan:2022nag} and from NNPDF~\cite{NNPDF:2024nan}, which differ
in how they treat the ingredients that were unknown at the time of the
respective fits, and they have been combined into a single
set~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}. An update of the MSHT analysis incorporating the
newer splitting-function and transition-matrix-element results finds changes to
the PDFs at most comparable to, and typically well below, the quoted
uncertainties, with still smaller effects on the parton
luminosities~\cite{Cridge:2025oel}. The differences these sets induce in the
Higgs cross sections are nevertheless significant, in several cases exceeding
the PDF uncertainty itself~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}.

How that mismatch is best estimated depends on the process, and in a way worth
stating here since the two production modes affected resolve it differently.
The most direct estimate is the difference between the NNLO and aN$^3$LO
predictions themselves, but that difference is only as meaningful as the
agreement between the two independent aN$^3$LO determinations. That agreement
is strongly process dependent: for vector-boson fusion the MSHT20 and NNPDF4.0
aN$^3$LO sets give cross sections agreeing to the quoted precision, while for
gluon fusion they differ by around $3\%$, on an effect of comparable size in
the two cases~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}. Where the two determinations agree the
direct difference is a meaningful measure of the effect in its own right; this
is how it is quantified for VBF in Section~\ref{sec:vbf_an3lo}, where it is
found to be comparable to the uncertainty already assigned and is therefore not
applied to the central prediction. Where they do not agree, a more conservative
prescription is required, and the treatment adopted for gluon fusion is set out
in Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}.
