\subsection{Summary of Theory Developments}
In this section we summarise the theory work carried out in recent years on
Higgs production via vector-boson fusion (VBF) at the LHC. A comparative study
of differential cross sections at fixed order, including parton-shower
corrections, has recently been made public~\cite{Barone:2025jey}. Besides
providing state-of-the-art differential predictions at fixed order and
recommendations for theory uncertainties in parton-shower simulations, it
contains a pedagogical description of higher-order corrections in VBF and a
review of the experimental and theoretical state of the art, with references
collected in its Tables~2 and~3, respectively. We therefore refer the interested
reader to Ref.~\cite{Barone:2025jey} and references therein. For fixed-order
calculations the Les Houches wishlist~\cite{Huss:2025nlt} is a further useful
resource. For completeness we nonetheless give a short review of recent and
state-of-the-art theory calculations for VBF at the LHC.

The electroweak (EW) production of a Higgs boson in association with two jets
(HJJ) comprises several contributions, of VBF and of VH type. The quality of
the VBF approximation, and the impact of higher-multiplicity contributions,
have been studied in the
literature~\cite{Figy:2003nv,Berger:2004pca,Ciccolini:2007jr,Bolzoni:2011cu,
Campanario:2013fsa,Campanario:2018ppz,Barone:2025jey}.

The state of the art for EW corrections is NLO
EW~\cite{Ciccolini:2007jr,Ciccolini:2007ec}, which are used in this article.
For QCD corrections the state of the art is NNLO QCD in the VBF approximation
at the differential level~\cite{Cacciari:2015jma,Cruz-Martinez:2018rod,Asteriadis:2021gpd}, while
inclusive corrections are available at N$^3$LO~\cite{Dreyer:2016oyx} in the structure function approximation~\cite{Han:1992hr,Bolzoni:2010xr,Bolzoni:2011cu} and are
used for the predictions presented here. Since the state-of-the-art QCD
computations are performed in the VBF approximation, which neglects the cross
talk between the two quark lines, the impact of non-factorisable corrections
has been studied in detail~\cite{Liu:2019tuy,Dreyer:2020urf,Asteriadis:2023nyl,
Bronnum-Hansen:2023vzh,Long:2023mvc,Gates:2023iiv}. In the predictions
presented here, non-factorisable corrections are accounted for as an uncertainty.

As mentioned above, the EW production of HJJ proceeds through several
mechanisms. In addition, separate contributions at different orders in
perturbation theory lead to the same final
state~\cite{Andersen:2006ag,Andersen:2007mp,Harlander:2008xn,Greiner:2016awe,
Greiner:2015jha,Andersen:2017kfc,Andersen:2018tnm,Andersen:2018kjg,
Chen:2021azt,Andersen:2022zte}; a summary of these irreducible backgrounds can
be found in Ref.~\cite{Barone:2025jey}. The characteristics of the VBF
production mechanism have also been studied in several phase-space regions,
including those in which the Higgs boson is
boosted~\cite{Becker:2020rjp,Buckley:2021gfw}. Finally, while historically the
focus of both theoretical and experimental work has been on a stable Higgs
boson, recent work~\cite{Asteriadis:2021gpd,Asteriadis:2024nbg} has shown that
considering the decay products of the Higgs boson along with its production
mechanism can induce non-trivial effects.

Beyond fixed order, significant work related to parton showers has been carried
out over the years. Parton-shower corrections matched to
QCD~\cite{Nason:2009ai,Frixione:2013mta} and even to EW~\cite{Jager:2022acp}
corrections are available at NLO accuracy, and multi-jet merged results have
been investigated~\cite{Hoche:2021mkv,Chen:2021phj}. Uncertainties related to
NLO QCD+PS simulations have been studied in
detail~\cite{Jager:2020hkz,Buckley:2021gfw,Hoche:2021mkv}, and recommendations
regarding such uncertainties are given in Ref.~\cite{Barone:2025jey}. Soft
physics effects such as multi-parton interactions and
hadronisation~\cite{Hoche:2021mkv,Bittrich:2021ztq} have been studied, as have
developments towards next-to-leading-logarithmic showers for
VBF~\cite{vanBeekveld:2023chs}.

\subsection{Inclusive Cross Section Predictions}
\label{sec:vbf_xs}

The results compiled in Tables~\ref{tab:vbf_XStot_7}-\ref{tab:vbf_XStot_14} are
combined according to
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:vbf-xsec-combined}
  \sigma^{\VBF} = \sigma_{\NNNLO}^{\DIS} (1+\delta_{\ELWK}) + \sigma_{\gamma} ,
\end{align}
%
where $\sigma_{\NNNLO}^{\DIS}$ is the $\NNNLO$ QCD cross section in the DIS
approximation. The relative EW corrections omitting the photon-induced
contributions are denoted by $\delta_{\ELWK}$, while the photon-induced
contributions are collected in $\sigma_{\gamma}$.

The $\NNNLO$ QCD cross section is obtained with
\texttt{proVBFH}~\cite{Cacciari:2015jma,Dreyer:2016oyx}. The NLO EW
corrections and the photon-induced contributions are computed with
{\sc Hawk}~\cite{Denner:2014cla}, as are the interferences between the $s$-,
$t$-, and $u$-channel contributions. It is worth mentioning that while
{\sc Hawk} takes all partonic channels into account at LO, at NLO the channels
featuring bottom quarks in the external state are omitted, which is a small
effect. The non-factorisable corrections are taken from
Refs.~\cite{Liu:2019tuy,Asteriadis:2023nyl}. The central renormalisation and
factorisation scales are set to the fixed value $\mu_F=\mu_R=M_W$; a fixed
scale is required for the EW corrections, and the same choice is retained for
the QCD contribution so that the two are combined consistently. The scale
uncertainty $\Delta_\mathrm{scale}$ quoted in the tables is obtained from a
3-point variation, in which $\mu_F$ and $\mu_R$ are varied together by a factor
of two up and down about this central value, the envelope of the three
resulting predictions being taken as the uncertainty. It is well below the
other uncertainties throughout, a consequence of the very mild scale dependence
of the inclusive cross section at N$^3$LO~\cite{Dreyer:2016oyx}.

The theory uncertainties are computed as
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:vbf-TU}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{TU}} = \max\left\{0.5\%,\delta_{\ELWK}^2\right\} + \frac{|\sigma_{\mbox{\scriptsize nf}}| + |\sigma_{\mbox{\scriptsize s/t/u}}|}{\sigma^{\VBF}}\%
\end{align}
%
where $\sigma_{\mbox{\scriptsize s/t/u}}$ refers to the size of the
interference contribution between $s$-, $t$- and $u$-channel diagrams for
$\sqrt{s}=\{13,13.6,14\}$ TeV. For the legacy numbers corresponding to
$\sqrt{s}=\{7,8\}$ TeV the non-factorisable contribution,
$\sigma_{\mbox{\scriptsize nf}}$, was not computed, and we instead set
\begin{align}
  \label{eq:vbf-TU-legacy}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{TU}} = \max\left[\max\left\{0.5\%,\delta_{\ELWK}^2\right\} + \frac{|\sigma_{\mbox{\scriptsize s/t/u}}|}{\sigma^{\VBF}}\%,1.0\%\right],
\end{align}
which in this case always corresponds to $1\%$.

\subsection{Parton distributions beyond NNLO}
\label{sec:vbf_an3lo}

The prediction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbf-xsec-combined} combines an N$^3$LO QCD
matrix element with the NNLO PDF4LHC21 parton distributions of
Section~\ref{sec:pdf}, and therefore carries the same mismatch in perturbative
accuracy as the gluon-fusion prediction, discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf}. To quantify it we have recomputed the
inclusive N$^3$LO cross section in the setup of this report, at
$\sqrt{s}=13$, $13.6$ and $14$~TeV, with the NNLO and approximate N$^3$LO
variants of the MSHT20xNNPDF40 combination of Ref.~\cite{Cridge:2024icl}.

Replacing PDF4LHC21 by the aN$^3$LO combination raises the cross
section by $3.3\%$ at $\sqrt{s}=13.6$~TeV. 
Writing $\sigma[S]$ for the N$^3$LO cross section evaluated with the PDF
set $S$, this shift separates exactly into
%
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\sigma[\mathrm{aN}^3\mathrm{LO}]-\sigma[\mathrm{PDF4LHC21}]}_{+3.3\%}
  = \underbrace{\sigma[\mathrm{NNLO}]-\sigma[\mathrm{PDF4LHC21}]}_{+1.1\%}
  + \underbrace{\sigma[\mathrm{aN}^3\mathrm{LO}]-\sigma[\mathrm{NNLO}]}_{+2.2\%}\,,
  \label{eq:vbf-pdf-th}
\end{equation}
%
where NNLO and aN$^3$LO denote the two corresponding members of the
MSHT20xNNPDF40 family and the percentages are relative to
$\sigma[\mathrm{PDF4LHC21}]$.
The first term is a change of PDF set at fixed NNLO accuracy, and is
already covered by the $\Delta_{\mathrm{PDF}\oplus\alphas}$ uncertainty
quoted in the tables; the second is a change of perturbative order within
one family, and is not.
Only the second term describes a genuine PDF-theory effect. Since the position of the working group is to use PDF4LHC21 at
NNLO as the baseline, we cannot advocate applying this shift directly
to the cross section. It is in any case comparable to the total
uncertainty of $^{+2.5}_{-2.4}\%$ already assigned to VBF at this
energy, and our central prediction and uncertainty therefore do not
include it. Should a more conservative uncertainty band be desired, we
recommend that the PDF-theory uncertainty of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbf-pdf-th},
$\Delta_{\mathrm{PDF-TH}} = \pm 2.2\%$, be added in quadrature to the
total uncertainty, across collider energies and Higgs-boson masses,
which brings the latter to approximately $\pm 3.3\%$. The energy dependence of the underlying shift is
negligible over the range considered, varying by less than $0.03$
percentage points between $13$ and $14$~TeV.

This estimate is taken directly from the difference between NNLO and aN$^3$LO
sets, rather than from the one-order-down proxy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ggf_pdf_th}
used for gluon fusion. The two prescriptions are appropriate in different
circumstances. A direct difference is only as reliable as the agreement between
the two independent aN$^3$LO PDF determinations, and for VBF that agreement is
good: the MSHT20 and NNPDF4.0 aN$^3$LO sets differ by $0.3\%$, against $1.4\%$
for the corresponding NNLO sets, so the two groups agree substantially better
at aN$^3$LO than at NNLO and the shift is well under control. For gluon fusion the
same two sets differ by around $3\%$ on an effect of comparable size, so the
direct difference is not a reliable estimator there; the prescription of
Section~\ref{sec:ggf_an3lo_pdf} is used instead, and the resulting uncertainty
is included in the gluon-fusion recommendation.
